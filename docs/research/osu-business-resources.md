## 2. Resource Details

### 2.1 OSU Advantage: IP and Licensing

OSU Advantage is OSU's commercialization and technology-transfer office. The issue calls it the Office of Commercialization and Corporate Development, and it now works under the OSU Advantage name.

- **What it offers:** Advice on who owns an idea, invention disclosure, patent and commercial-viability assessment, and licensing. For startups it offers "fair and reasonable licensing terms," often with deferred fees, reduced royalties, and equity-based arrangements.
- **Eligibility:** OSU faculty, staff, and students.
- **Cost:** No fee to talk to them.
- **Deadlines:** None. The office says the best time to contact them is **before any public disclosure**, which includes publications, posters, presentations, and thesis defenses. For this team, that means before the capstone Expo and before promoting the public repository.
- **Contact:** OSU Advantage, A312 Kerr Administration Building, Corvallis, OR 97331. Phone 541-737-3888. Use the "I Have an Idea" portal on the website to start a disclosure.
- **Source:** [Oregon State University Intellectual Property Policy](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/ip-licensing/oregon-state-university-ip-policy)

### 2.2 OSU Advantage Accelerator

The Advantage Accelerator is OSU's startup accelerator. It runs a series of programs, each leading into the next: Iterate, then I-Corps, then Accelerate, then Launch.

- **Contact for all programs:** OSU Advantage Accelerator, 1110 NE Circle Blvd, Corvallis, OR 97333. Phone (541) 713-2024. Email OSUAdvantage@oregonstate.edu, or use the "Contact Our Team" form on the website.
- **Sources:** [Advantage Accelerator](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/advantage-accelerator) and [Programs](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/advantage-accelerator/programs)

#### 2.2.1 Iterate

- **What it offers:** A 1-hour interactive Zoom workshop. It covers how a startup differs from an idea, how to find a problem worth solving, the Value Proposition Canvas, how to pitch, and how OSU licensing managers support commercialization.
- **Eligibility:** Faculty researchers, graduate students, postdocs, staff, and community members. The page doesn't mention undergraduates.
- **Cost:** No fee listed.
- **Dates:** Thursday, January 14, 2027, or March 3, 2027, both 11:00 a.m.–12:00 p.m. PT. No registration deadline is listed.
- **How to apply:** Register through the [Microsoft Forms link](https://forms.office.com/r/EULSFYT0Us) on the program page.
- **Source:** [Iterate](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/advantage-accelerator/programs/iterate)

#### 2.2.2 OSU Regional NSF I-Corps

- **What it offers:** Training for early-stage teams to define a value proposition and find a target customer segment through customer discovery. Each team conducts 20 customer interviews with hands-on guidance from instructors. Teams with university IP that finish with instructor endorsement can apply to the national NSF I-Corps program. That program runs 7 weeks and comes with a $50,000 NSF grant.
- **Eligibility:** A team of at least 2 people. Preference goes to university-affiliated early-stage startups, and community teams are considered. No business experience is needed. Every member must attend all sessions and spend 2–5 hours a week outside sessions. The page lists faculty, graduate students, postdocs, and community members, and it doesn't say whether undergraduates can join.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Dates:** Fall term sessions are October 6, 13, 20, and 27. Winter term sessions are January 12, 19, 26, and February 2. All sessions run 2–4 p.m. PT on Zoom. The page says Winter applications are open and gives no deadline.
- **How to apply:** Submit the online application on the program page, which takes about 10 minutes.
- **Source:** [OSU Regional NSF I-Corps Program](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/advantage-accelerator/nsf-i-corps-agtech)

#### 2.2.3 Accelerate

- **What it offers:** A 5-week program with weekly sessions: business model, speed mentoring, how to sell, go-to-market, and speed pitching to industry experts.
- **Eligibility:** Open to students, researchers, staff, and community members who have completed the OSU Regional NSF I-Corps program. Ventures must show growth potential and intent to create jobs, and participants agree to a participant agreement.
- **Cost:** No fee is stated for OSU-affiliated participants. Participants not affiliated with OSU may give 1% equity plus a 2.5% royalty on sales once annual revenue reaches $200,000, capped at $100,000 in total.
- **Dates:** Runs three times a year. The current cohort meets Tuesdays, November 3 to December 1, 2026, 2–4 p.m. PT on Zoom.
- **How to apply:** Invite only. Submit the interest form on the program page.
- **Source:** [Accelerate](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/advantage-accelerator/programs/accelerate)

The Accelerator also runs **Launch**, a 5-month invite-only program for startups ready to scale, and **Moxie**, a 15-month program for faculty and postdocs. Neither applies to this team yet.

### 2.3 Next Great Startup (InnovationX)

The Next Great Startup is OSU's annual competition for student-led teams turning a startup idea into a company. It is run by InnovationX, the College of Business center for innovation and entrepreneurship, with OSU Advantage.

- **What it offers:** Cash prizes, donated legal and accounting services, industry and venture-capital mentors, and idea-development and pitch-preparation workshops. As an example, the 2017 cycle awarded more than $30,000, including $12,000 cash and $6,000 in legal services for first place. Current prize amounts weren't published.
- **Eligibility:** Undergraduate and graduate students of all majors. A current OSU student in a degree program and in good academic standing must lead the team. Any type of business idea is eligible.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Deadlines:** The 2026–27 timeline was not published at the time of research. In past cycles, colleges held preliminary rounds first (Engineering and Business have run a joint one), and campus-wide applications were due between mid-January and late February. The competition runs over two terms in three rounds: preliminaries, semifinals, and finals.
- **Contact:** NextGreatStartup@oregonstate.edu. InnovationX phone 541-737-2135.
- **Sources:** [Next Great Startup](https://business.oregonstate.edu/nextgreatstartup), [Student Resources](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/innovation-impact-program/student-resources), [2017 competition results](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/feature-story/next-great-startup-more-competition-it%E2%80%99s-proving-ground)

### 2.4 Advantage Accelerator Internship

- **What it offers:** A paid student job researching the commercial potential of business ideas from the OSU community, including market research, competitive analysis, and due diligence. It is a way for team members to learn how commercialization works and to meet the Accelerator staff.
- **Eligibility:** OSU students. Up to 10 hours a week during the academic year and up to 30 hours a week in summer.
- **Cost:** None; it is a paid position.
- **Deadlines:** No fixed deadline. Openings have been advertised through the Honors College and the Accelerator.
- **Contact:** Advantage Accelerator, (541) 713-2024.
- **Sources:** [Student Resources](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/innovation-impact-program/student-resources), [Advantage Accelerator interns](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/feature-story/advantage-accelerator-interns-driven-succeed)

### 2.5 Other Supporting Resources

- **College I&E representatives:** Each college has an innovation and entrepreneurship contact. See the [Innovation Advocates directory](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/innovation-impact-program/contact/innovation-advocates) for the College of Engineering contact.
- **InnovationX classes and degrees:** The College of Business offers an [Innovation and Entrepreneurship minor](http://business.oregonstate.edu/programs/undergraduate/innovation-entrepreneurship) open to non-business majors.
- **DAMLab makerspace:** A College of Business makerspace for prototyping hardware, such as a drone mount or a sensor rig. See the [DAMLab page](https://business.oregonstate.edu/student-experience/resources/DAMlab).
- **Student resources hub:** OSU Advantage keeps an up-to-date list at [Student Resources](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/innovation-impact-program/student-resources).

## 3. Capstone IP Policy

### 3.1 What the Policies Say

- **EECS capstone program:** The project-submission page for capstone partners says: "Students own any IP resulting from the project. If your organization would like to retain IP rights, indicate that when submitting your project." It also notes that partners may be asked to sign an NDA. Source: [EECS Capstone Project Submission](https://eecs.engineering.oregonstate.edu/capstone/submission/pages/)
- **OSU IP Policy (University Policy 06-200, effective April 12, 2023):** "In most cases, non-employee students own the intellectual property they make at OSU." Students may have to assign IP to OSU if it was made "with substantial use of OSU resources or as part of research projects with contractual obligations related to intellectual property." Students can also choose to assign IP to OSU, and are then treated like OSU employees. Source: [Oregon State University Intellectual Property Policy](https://advantage.oregonstate.edu/ip-licensing/oregon-state-university-ip-policy)
